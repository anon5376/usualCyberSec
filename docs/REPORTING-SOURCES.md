# Reporting and recovery source notes

The app links to the following official platform help routes. Each route record was checked on 2026-10-22. These pages describe possible reporting or recovery processes; they do not establish that any particular content violates policy or promise removal, restored access, or a response time.

| Service and use | Exact route linked by the app | What the public source supports | Access and scope caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube: privacy complaint involving synthetic or altered face/voice likeness | [Protecting your identity](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801895?hl=en) | A uniquely identifiable subject or legal representative can request review. YouTube considers realism, disclosure, consent, parody/satire, and public interest. | The process is a review; removal is not automatic. The page links to YouTube’s separate Privacy Complaint Process. |
| Instagram: impersonating account | [Report an impersonation account](https://help.instagram.com/370054663112398/) | Instagram’s official Help Center identifies an in-app or online impersonation report route. | The page returned an automated-access restriction during source checking. The eSafety Commissioner’s [Instagram guide](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/esafety-guide/instagram) corroborates this exact official link, but does not verify the current form fields. Identity requirements remain unknown. |
| Facebook: profile or Page impersonation | [Report an impersonating profile or Page](https://www.facebook.com/help/174210519303259) | This is Facebook’s official Help Center route for reports involving profiles or Pages pretending to be someone else. | Logged-out access redirected to Facebook login during source checking. Current form fields, identity-document requirements, and account-access requirements were not verified. |
| TikTok: AI-generated or manipulated post | [About AI-generated content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/ai-generated-content/) | TikTok’s policy guidance says users can report posts that may violate its AI-generated/edited-media rules, including AI impersonation. Reporting labels and policy context matter. | The help page redirected to a TikTok support page that this reader could not open. Treat the current in-app/browser report categories as authoritative; AI generation alone is not a violation finding. |
| TikTok: impersonating account | [Report an impersonation account](https://www.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/report-a-problem/report-an-impersonation-account) | TikTok’s public guidance describes reporting from a profile and separate U.S. and non-U.S. online processes. Its U.S. form asks for email, country, who is impersonated, valid ID, confirmation, and electronic signature; its non-U.S. form uses report-topic/category/reason choices. | The user must choose the appropriate region-specific route. usualCyberSec does not collect ID or decide which route applies. |
| X: impersonation | [Report impersonation accounts](https://help.x.com/en/safety-and-security/report-x-impersonation) | X describes routes for the affected person or authorized representative and for bystanders; the help article says an X account is not generally required to report impersonation. | The dedicated self-report flow may require sign-in or identity information, depending on the path. The app does not collect either. |
| Google Account: compromise | [Secure a hacked or compromised Google Account](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6294825?hl=en) | If unable to sign in, Google directs the account owner to account recovery. Its guidance recommends ownership details and, where possible, a familiar device/location, then reviewing devices and security activity. | Work or school accounts may require an organization administrator. Recovery depends on Google’s own ownership checks. |
| Microsoft Account: personal account recovery | [Help with the Microsoft account recovery form](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/help-with-the-microsoft-account-recovery-form) | Microsoft describes a personal-account recovery form, a reachable alternate contact email, and account-history questions. Familiar devices and locations are recommended. | Microsoft says the form cannot recover an account with two-step verification enabled when none of its alternate methods are available. Work or school accounts use administrator support. |

The eSafety Commissioner source is an Australian public safety guide, used only to corroborate the Instagram link; it does not make the platform route Australia-specific. App route records include a region note where the source supports one. They do not provide location-aware selection, jurisdiction-specific legal advice, or a complete list of country-specific forms. Platforms can change their pages and requirements after the recorded check date.

## Packet behavior

The local text packet adds the selected route’s public URL, summary, requirements, steps, region and source-check date. It always includes incident category, status, and local created/updated timestamps. Case title, summary, source URLs, fingerprints, filenames, and notes are optional fields; all begin unchecked. The preview is local, the download is plaintext, raw media files are excluded, and usualCyberSec never submits the packet.
